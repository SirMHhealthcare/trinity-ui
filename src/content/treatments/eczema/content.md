## What is Eczema?

Eczema is a common inflammatory skin condition that causes **dryness, itching, redness, irritation and sometimes cracking or oozing of the skin**. It can affect people of any age, including infants and adults.

Eczema often follows a pattern of **flare-ups and periods of improvement**. The symptoms and appearance can vary depending on the type of eczema, age of the patient and individual triggers.
Eczema is not contagious. It cannot spread from one person to another through touching or sharing household items.

## Common Symptoms of Eczema

- Dry and sensitive skin
- Intense itching
- Red or inflamed skin
- Rough or scaly patches
- Burning or irritation
- Cracked skin
- Small bumps or blisters
- Oozing or crusting in some cases
- Thickened skin after repeated scratching

Itching is often one of the most troublesome symptoms and may become worse at night, affecting sleep.

## Common Types of Eczema

### Atopic Dermatitis
Atopic dermatitis is the most common form of eczema. It frequently begins during childhood but can continue into adulthood or develop later in life.
It may be associated with other allergic conditions such as **allergic rhinitis and asthma**.

### Contact Dermatitis
Contact dermatitis occurs when the skin reacts to a substance that comes into contact with it. Common irritants or allergens include soaps, detergents, cosmetics, fragrances, metals and certain chemicals.

### Dyshidrotic Eczema

This type mainly affects the **hands and feet** and may produce small, itchy blisters.

### Nummular Eczema
Nummular eczema produces **round or coin-shaped itchy patches** on the skin.

### Seborrheic Dermatitis
Seborrheic dermatitis commonly affects areas rich in oil glands, including the **scalp, eyebrows, sides of the nose and ears**. It can cause scaling and redness and is sometimes associated with dandruff.

## What Causes Eczema?

There is no single cause of eczema. It can develop because of a combination of genetic, immune-system and skin-barrier factors.

People with eczema often have a weakened skin barrier, allowing the skin to lose moisture more easily and become more sensitive to irritants.

Common factors that may trigger or worsen symptoms include:

- Dry weather
- Excessive heat
- Sweating
- Soaps and detergents
- Fragrances
- Dust
- Certain fabrics
- Stress
- Skin infections
- Environmental changes
- Certain foods in some individuals

## Eczema and Allergy

Eczema can occur alongside allergic conditions such as asthma and allergic rhinitis, particularly in people with atopic dermatitis.
